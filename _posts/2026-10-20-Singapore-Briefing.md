## THE GROUND PICTURE

The most immediate disruption to daily life in Singapore is the return of severe transboundary haze. Air quality has fluctuated into unhealthy ranges, prompting the **Ministry of Health (MOH)** to release N95 masks from the national stockpile to major supermarkets like **FairPrice**, **Cold Storage**, and **Sheng Siong**. The **Ministry of Manpower (MOM)** has issued strict guidelines advising employers to reduce outdoor work as PM2.5 levels rise, and to stop outdoor work for vulnerable employees entirely if hourly PM2.5 concentrations exceed 250. 

At the same time, daily operational costs are rising. Commuters are facing higher public transport fares, while taxi and ride-hailing fares have surged nearly 50 percent over the last five years. These physical and financial pressures are creating a more challenging day-to-day environment for working professionals, even as the broader economy shows signs of growth.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Singapore’s gross domestic product grew by 5.4 percent in the third quarter of 2026, but this growth is accompanied by persistent domestic inflation. Core inflation rose by 2.2 percent in August, driven by rising costs in food, services, and energy. Spot electricity prices have hit a yearly high, which is expected to translate into higher household utility bills in the coming months. 

The cost stack for households remains heavy. To cushion the impact on lower-income families, the **FairPrice Group** has frozen prices on basic items like chicken and eggs until the end of 2026 and doubled **Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS)** discounts at its pharmacies. Meanwhile, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** has maintained the prevailing rate of appreciation for the Singapore dollar to keep import inflation in check, warning that global supply chain disruptions could still trigger price spikes. 

The core economic dynamic is one of strong macroeconomic growth that is not yet translating into relief for household budgets.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The employment landscape is experiencing uneven growth and structural disruption. The **National Wages Council (NWC)** has recommended a 6 to 8 percent salary increase for lower-wage workers to help them cope with inflation. However, professional and tech sectors are facing headwinds, with global firms like **Qualcomm** and **Ubisoft** executing performance-focused layoffs that have affected local headcounts. 

For entry-level professionals, the rapid adoption of generative artificial intelligence is reshaping hiring. To protect the pipeline of young talent, the government is offering 3,000 year-long internships to tertiary students to help them gain practical experience before entering a shifting job market. Additionally, local telecom giant **Singtel** was fined S$1 million by the **Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)** for a major three-day mobile service disruption in March, highlighting the operational risks that accompany highly automated digital infrastructure.

The core employment dynamic is a tightening market for corporate and tech roles as automation reduces the demand for traditional entry-level tasks.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant regulatory energy and funding toward digital safety, data sovereignty, and energy security. A new licensing framework has been introduced requiring all data center operators in Singapore with a critical IT load of at least 3 megawatts to be licensed. The **Ministry of Communications and Information** is also studying stronger safeguards for high-risk AI uses as systems become more autonomous. 

To support the aging population, the government is focusing on longevity policies. The **Central Provident Fund (CPF)** maintained its Grade A rating in global pension rankings, and the government is encouraging older residents to remain in the workforce longer. For long-term resource security, Singapore is actively exploring small modular nuclear reactors to diversify its energy grid away from fossil fuels and meet the massive power demands of local data centers.

The core government direction is the tightening of regulatory control over digital infrastructure and AI safety while securing long-term energy alternatives.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore’s economic relationship with its immediate neighbors is evolving rapidly through new infrastructure and shared environmental challenges. Planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone** is nearing completion, with a potential launch in March. The **Johor-Singapore RTS Link** is also progressing, with fares expected to be announced by the end of the year, though local retailers worry the link will shift significant consumer spending from Singapore to Johor Bahru.

Bilateral tensions have surfaced over the transboundary haze. The **National Environment Agency (NEA)** has issued preventive legal notices under the **Transboundary Haze Pollution Act** to several Indonesian agro-industrial firms linked to forest fires. While Indonesia has deployed security forces to combat the fires, its initial reluctance to accept foreign firefighting assistance has prolonged the air quality crisis across the region.

The core regional dynamic is a push for deeper economic integration with Malaysia alongside regulatory friction with Indonesia over environmental enforcement.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical and technology rivalries are directly impacting Singapore’s corporate landscape. The United States government’s ban on the Chinese AI tool **DeepSeek** for federal agencies, alongside ongoing sanctions against Chinese tech entities, is forcing multinational corporations to review their software and supply chain dependencies. 

Singapore is positioning itself as a neutral, highly regulated harbor amidst these tensions. This has attracted companies like Danish jewelry brand **Pandora**, which is establishing a new regional headquarters at Marina Bay to expand its operations outside of China. Similarly, semiconductor firm **Mi Material** is leveraging Singapore's stable environment to launch a S$215 million initial public offering on the Singapore Exchange to fund its expansion into both the United States and Malaysia.
