## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singapore is experiencing a dual-speed reality. While the artificial intelligence boom is driving factory output and financial market rallies, local workers face a five-year high in retrenchments. Outward-facing sectors are restructuring, leading to high-profile layoffs at institutions like **GovTech** and major automotive distributors.

At the same time, the daily cost stack remains heavy. High rental rates, rising utility bills, and elevated transport costs are squeezing middle-income households. This financial pressure is reflected in reports that sixty percent of Singaporeans now hold a second job to supplement their primary income.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost of living in Singapore remains high, driven by global energy volatility and domestic overheads. The **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** has allowed the Singapore Dollar to strengthen to shield the country from import inflation caused by the ongoing war in Iran. While core inflation moderated slightly to 1.6 percent in June, households continue to feel the pinch of high electricity bills and daily expenses.

The housing market shows mixed signals. Public housing resale prices eased slightly by 0.3 percent in the second quarter, but private home rentals rose by 1.0 percent in the first half of the year. High commercial rents are also reshaping the retail landscape, forcing established brands like **Metro** department store to close outlets while driving a wave of closures among local food and beverage businesses.

High domestic operating costs and global energy risks are keeping the cost of living elevated despite government efforts to curb inflation through a strong currency.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The employment landscape is tightening as companies restructure to cope with rising costs and technological shifts. Retrenchments reached 4,500 in the second quarter, driven by outward-facing businesses. Tech professionals face immediate pressure, highlighted by ninety-three job cuts at **GovTech** and intense competition for entry-level roles despite thousands of vacant positions.

Traditional sectors are also shedding headcount. Automotive distributors, including **Borneo Motors**, **Motor Image**, and **Tan Chong Motor**, have implemented layoffs as part of corporate restructuring. Meanwhile, workplace burnout is highly prevalent, with sixty-one percent of workers reporting exhaustion and one-third of junior lawyers expressing a desire to leave their profession.

Restructuring in tech and automotive sectors is driving retrenchments to a five-year high even as specialized AI and semiconductor roles see continued investment.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant resources toward infrastructure resilience and workforce adaptability. Under the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise 2030 (RIE2030)** plan, Singapore will invest 800 million dollars in transport research and autonomous vehicle integration. To support workers through these transitions, the government is launching a driver transition package and establishing a new **Skills and Workforce Development Agency** later this year.

Regulatory oversight is tightening across daily life and digital spaces. The **Health Sciences Authority (HSA)** is intensifying its crackdown on vaping, while the **Online Safety Commission** has begun tackling online harms and doxing. Additionally, the **Cat Management Framework** transition period ends in August, making microchipping and licensing mandatory for pet owners.

The government is prioritizing long-term infrastructure research and structured workforce retraining while tightening domestic regulations on public safety and digital health.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic relationship with its neighbors is deepening, particularly through the development of the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)**. The upcoming **Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** is already transforming Johor Bahru's commercial landscape, though it has sparked debates in Singapore over whether rental controls are needed to protect local heartland shops from cross-border competition.

Regionally, Malaysia is rapidly emerging as a major competitor in the AI infrastructure race, securing massive investments for data centers in Johor. Meanwhile, Indonesia is focusing on domestic reforms, including restructuring its state programs and managing forest fires exacerbated by El Niño, which could threaten regional air quality.

Deepening physical integration with Malaysia offers long-term economic benefits but intensifies regional competition for data center infrastructure and retail capital.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The 150-day war in Iran is directly impacting Singapore's supply chains and energy security. While fuel supplies remain steady, shipping risks in the Strait of Hormuz are keeping global oil prices volatile and raising insurance premiums for regional maritime routes. This conflict has also forced the Singapore government to defer its scheduled political salary review until the economic fallout becomes clearer.

Simultaneously, the joint currency intervention by the United States and Japan has pushed the Japanese Yen higher against the Singapore Dollar. This shift is altering import costs and affecting local investment portfolios. Furthermore, ongoing trade tensions and US technology restrictions are forcing multinational corporations to adjust their supply chains, driving semiconductor manufacturers like **UMC** to expand their cleanroom capacity in Singapore.

---
