## THE GROUND PICTURE

Living and working in Singapore right now means navigating a high-cost environment with a tightening job market. While the economy continues to grow, ordinary professionals are feeling the squeeze of persistent inflation and corporate restructuring. Global conflicts and new trade barriers are directly impacting local prices and business operations.

The employment landscape is shifting. Salary disputes and wrongful dismissal claims have reached their highest levels since 2019. At the same time, fresh graduates are finding it difficult to secure entry-level roles in their preferred sectors, forcing many to adjust their career expectations. 

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Singapore’s core inflation rose to 1.6% in June, driven by higher costs for food, retail goods, and services. The overall cost stack remains heavy for households. Car ownership costs are exceptionally high, and the **Category A Certificate of Entitlement (COE)** supply will be cut further from August to October, even though the overall quota will see a marginal increase of 0.2%. 

Energy costs are under constant pressure as global oil prices hover above US$100 per barrel due to ongoing conflicts in the Middle East. In response to these global cost pressures, the government brought forward the release of **CDC vouchers** by seven months. Additionally, nearly 13,000 low-income households will receive public transport vouchers to help offset rising transit fares. 

The financial sector is showing mixed signals. While average wealth in Singapore is climbing, this growth is heavily driven by exposure to financial assets rather than ordinary savings. Local banks are expected to post loan growth, but their profit margins are projected to dip in the second half of the year. 

The core dynamic at work is that household budgets are being squeezed by a combination of structural domestic costs and volatile global energy prices.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market is experiencing significant friction as companies reorganize to cope with rising costs and technological change. Employment claims filed with the **Tripartite Alliance for Dispute Management (TADM)** have spiked. This rise is directly linked to higher job turnover from retrenchments and business closures across various sectors. 

The technology sector is undergoing a visible transition. **GovTech** recently cut 93 roles in a restructuring exercise to shift toward more technical and outcome-driven functions. Furthermore, local workers are reportedly less optimistic about the impact of artificial intelligence on their jobs compared to their peers in the region, citing concerns over displacement and skill obsolescence. 

Despite these cuts, some areas continue to expand. Financial institutions like **JPMorgan** and **UOB** are expanding their wealth management and private banking teams in Singapore. However, the overall hiring sentiment remains cautious, and employers are increasingly utilizing AI to automate customer service and administrative tasks.

The core dynamic is that job security is declining as employers restructure operations and adopt automation to manage rising business costs.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant funding and regulatory attention toward digital safety, transport infrastructure, and energy security. The newly established **Online Safety Commission** handled 200 cases in its first month of operation, signaling a much tougher stance on online harms like doxing. The government is also reviewing social media safeguards for children, opting for targeted system designs rather than blanket bans.

In infrastructure and research, the state is investing S$800 million in transport research over the next five years under the **RIE2030** plan. This includes a S$4 billion investment to revamp the Changi Airport control tower and upgrade air traffic systems. Reflecting the critical importance of resource security, the Ministry of Trade and Industry is set to be renamed the **Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry**.

To support workers through technological transitions, the government is launching a new **Skills and Workforce Development Agency** later this year. It has also introduced career conversion programmes and training incentives of up to S$1,600 for point-to-point drivers to prepare them for the eventual transition to autonomous vehicles.

The core direction is that the government is actively funding deep-tech infrastructure and tightening digital regulations to secure Singapore's long-term economic resilience.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic ties with its immediate neighbors to navigate domestic land and labor constraints. Planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a potential launch targeted for March. This zone is expected to reshape cross-border shopping, business operations, and talent flows. 

Regional competition for tech talent is intensifying. East Asian economies, including Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan, are actively offering specialized tech visas and scholarships to attract ASEAN professionals. Meanwhile, Singapore food firms are increasingly shifting their mass production lines to Malaysia while keeping their high-value research and development operations local. 

Geopolitical developments are also shifting trade routes. Thailand has decided to scrap its proposed US$38.3 billion land bridge project following a commercial viability study. This decision preserves the strategic dominance of the Malacca Strait and Singapore's position as the primary maritime gateway for the region.

The core dynamic is that Singapore is integrating more closely with Malaysia to build a shared economic ecosystem while defending its status as the region's primary high-value hub.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The United States has imposed a new 12.5% tariff on Singapore exports, citing concerns over forced labor in global supply chains. While economists suggest the immediate macroeconomic impact on Singapore will be contained due to exemptions for semiconductors and electronics, the tariff affects about one-third of domestic exports to the US. The **Ministry of Trade and Industry** is actively engaging with US officials to find a resolution.

Simultaneously, the ongoing conflict in the Middle East is directly impacting local consumer prices. Ongoing attacks on shipping in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz have forced global tankers to take longer routes, driving up jet fuel and shipping costs. This has led carriers like **Singapore Airlines** to increase passenger and cargo fuel surcharges, which will keep import costs and travel expenses high for local consumers.
