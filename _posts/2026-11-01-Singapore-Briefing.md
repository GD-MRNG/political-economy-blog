## THE GROUND PICTURE

Living and working in Singapore right now means navigating a dual reality of steady macroeconomic growth alongside rising personal costs and workplace stress. While overall employment continues to expand, retrenchments have climbed to a near six-year high, driven largely by corporate reorganisations. This creates a more cautious job market, particularly for mid-career professionals and those in the technology sector.

At the same time, daily expenses are rising. Everyday costs like transport and dining out are taking up a larger share of household budgets. The physical environment also demands attention, as the threat of transboundary haze persists, prompting stronger regulatory actions from the government against errant firms and driving public discussions on long-term energy and environmental resilience.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Singapore’s core inflation eased to 2% in September, prompting the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** to maintain the prevailing rate of appreciation for the Singapore Dollar to keep import inflation at bay. Despite this easing, the cost stack for residents remains heavy. Taxi and ride-hailing fares have surged nearly 50% over the past five years, and daily food costs are rising as hawkers adjust to high operating costs by raising prices, reducing portions, or altering ingredients. To help lower-income families, the **FairPrice Group** has doubled discounts for **CHAS Blue and Orange** cardholders and capped prices on essential items like eggs and chicken.

In the property market, dynamics are highly fragmented. The private residential sector saw strong demand at the launch of **Thomson Reserve**, which sold 90% of its units at an average of S$2,900 per square foot. However, one in five prime-region private home resale transactions in the third quarter of 2026 was sold at a loss. In the public housing market, million-dollar **HDB** resale transactions reached a record high of 597 in the third quarter, even as overall HDB resale prices dipped slightly by 0.1%. To cool the automotive market, the government extended loan limits on private-hire car buyers, though motor dealers expect this will have a limited immediate impact on **Certificate of Entitlement (COE)** prices.

The core dynamic at work is a highly bifurcated market where premium property and asset prices remain elevated while everyday operational and living costs continue to squeeze household budgets.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The local labour market expanded in the third quarter of 2026, but this growth was accompanied by rising retrenchments, which reached 4,800 cases. Layoffs were primarily driven by business reorganisation, with notable cuts occurring at **GovTech**, which executed its second wave of layoffs. Burnout remains a significant issue, with a reported 61% of Singapore workers experiencing workplace exhaustion. For lower-wage workers, the **National Wages Council (NWC)** has recommended a 6% to 8% built-in wage increase, and bus captains are set to receive a starting salary increase of S$450 from 2027.

Technology adoption is rapidly reshaping human resource practices and operational workflows. A survey by the **Singapore National Employers Federation (SNEF)** revealed that nearly half of employers using artificial intelligence in HR deploy it for recruitment, though over 80% of these processes still require human oversight. Meanwhile, the legal sector is facing talent retention challenges, with one in three new lawyers considering leaving the industry due to high burnout rates. 

The core dynamic is a tightening job market where employment growth is offset by restructuring-driven layoffs and high rates of employee burnout.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant regulatory energy and funding toward security, digital infrastructure, and energy transition. To combat rampant impersonation scams, the **Singapore Police Force** has consolidated its official communications under a single caller ID, **6000 0999**, and parliament has introduced laws granting the police broader powers to order banks and telecommunications companies to disable scam accounts. In the technology sector, a new licensing law has been implemented for data centres with a critical IT load of 3 megawatts or more, reflecting a push for tighter governance over high-capacity digital infrastructure.

To accelerate industrial AI adoption, **Enterprise Singapore (EnterpriseSG)** has allocated S$45 million to help advanced manufacturing small and medium enterprises integrate AI tools into their operations. On the energy front, the **Energy Market Authority (EMA)** has launched a five-year Energy Infrastructure Plan to upgrade the grid and has appointed nuclear consultants to prepare Singapore for a 2027 safety mission by the **International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA)**. Additionally, the EMA is conducting feasibility studies on geothermal energy following surveys that identified deep resources.

The core direction is a push toward strict regulatory oversight of digital infrastructure and scams, paired with heavy state funding for industrial AI adoption and clean energy exploration.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic integration with Malaysia through the upcoming **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)**, which is nearing completion and expected to launch in March. This initiative is driving substantial cross-border banking activity, prompting **OCBC** to support Malaysian Ringgit transactions in its savings accounts and **Maybank** to launch dedicated Ringgit accounts for corporate clients. Furthermore, **DBS**, Maybank, and the **International Finance Corporation (IFC)** are collaborating on a S$7.7 billion solar project financing deal in Johor.

Bilateral relations with Indonesia have faced minor friction over resource supply and environmental accountability. Indonesia’s decision to cut gas exports to Singapore raised energy concerns, though the EMA confirmed that Singapore has sufficient alternative supplies and that the cuts represent less than 2% of daily demand. On transboundary haze, the **National Environment Agency (NEA)** has intensified its investigations, issuing legal notices to ten Singapore-based agribusiness firms linked to Indonesian forest fires, with five of these companies agreeing to independent assessments.

The core dynamic is a deepening financial and infrastructural tie with Malaysia via the JS-SEZ, alongside regulatory friction with Indonesia over gas supplies and haze accountability.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global technology volatility and geopolitical tensions are directly impacting Singapore’s industrial and financial sectors. A tech stock sell-off on Wall Street triggered a decline in local semiconductor stocks, such as **AEM**, highlighting Singapore's exposure to global hardware market cycles. Additionally, the massive US$6.3 billion cyber heist at **Binance**, which the **FBI** attributed to North Korean state-backed hackers, forced the exchange to temporarily suspend withdrawals, directly affecting local cryptocurrency investors before services were resumed in phases.

Geopolitical scrutiny has also landed on Singapore-based entities. The US government is investigating reports that **Nvidia** CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a Chinese spy plot, with scrutiny extending to Singapore-based **Megaspeed International** due to its alleged connections to the case. These developments emphasize Singapore's position as a high-stakes node in the ongoing US-China technology conflict, where local firms face increased compliance and security risks.
