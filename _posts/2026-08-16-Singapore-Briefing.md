## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singapore is experiencing a dual reality of strong macroeconomic growth and intense local cost pressures. The economy expanded by 6.2 percent in the second quarter, driven by a global surge in demand for artificial intelligence and electronics. Yet, this growth has not fully translated into daily financial relief for residents, who continue to face high living costs and a shifting job market.

The physical and digital infrastructure of the city is changing rapidly. The upcoming **Johor-Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** is forcing local businesses to prepare for a future where Johor Bahru is only a five-minute train ride away. At the same time, the rapid integration of artificial intelligence is reshaping workplaces, creating anxiety over job security even as overall employment numbers rise.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost of daily life in Singapore remains high, prompting significant government intervention. To help households cope, the government is distributing **Community Development Council (CDC) Vouchers** worth $500 and preparing a $1 billion support package that includes cash payouts of up to $600 in September. The **Monetary Authority of Singapore** has allowed the Singapore dollar to strengthen to shield the economy from imported inflation, particularly volatile oil prices driven by tensions in the Strait of Hormuz.

In the housing market, demand for older **Housing & Development Board (HDB)** flats remains exceptionally high, while the private residential market shows signs of cooling, with two-thirds of Sentosa Cove resales transacting at a loss. To assist buyers, the government has lifted the wait-out period for private homeowners buying HDB resale flats and introduced a new minimum transaction rule to protect consumers. Meanwhile, retail and food businesses are struggling with high operating costs, leading to a rise in budget-friendly dining options under $10 and prompting some consumers to shift their spending to Malaysia.

The material cost of living is being heavily managed through government subsidies and currency strength to offset global supply shocks and domestic price pressures.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

Singapore's job market is expanding, but structural shifts are causing localized pain. While the manufacturing sector is rebounding strongly, retrenchments are rising in outward-oriented sectors due to corporate restructuring. Fresh graduates are finding the job hunt difficult, with one in five from autonomous universities still seeking employment several months after graduation.

The technology sector is undergoing a transition as companies adjust to artificial intelligence. **GovTech** is restructuring and retrenching some officers to streamline digital services, while global tech giants are balancing heavy AI infrastructure spending with disciplined headcount management. Employees are increasingly concerned about AI screening job applications and the potential for technology to reduce entry-level learning opportunities.

Employment is growing overall, but workers face heightened insecurity as companies restructure roles and adopt automation to manage rising costs.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant policy energy and funding toward long-term social stability and economic compliance. A dedicated marriage and parenthood workgroup, led by Minister Indranee Rajah, is preparing new family support measures to address falling birth rates, with initial plans set for the National Day Rally. To secure future economic competitiveness, the government is investing $800 million in transport research and innovation under the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise 2030 (RIE2030)** plan.

Regulatory oversight is also tightening across multiple sectors. The **Ministry of Trade and Industry** is enforcing stricter rules on transshipment firms, requiring them to declare the true country of origin of goods to maintain Singapore's reputation as a trusted trade hub. Additionally, the **National Environment Agency** has defended the integrity of its **Beverage Container Return Scheme**, confirming that $550,000 in deposits have been successfully refunded to consumers despite online misinformation.

The state is focusing resources on boosting demographic resilience, upgrading transport infrastructure, and tightening regulatory compliance to protect Singapore's international standing.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic relationship with its immediate neighbors is entering a highly integrated phase. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone** is nearing completion, which both countries hope will create a shared manufacturing and technology ecosystem. However, this integration presents a challenge for Singapore merchants, who must upgrade their offerings as more local consumers consider traveling to Johor Bahru for cheaper retail and dining options.

Further abroad, Southeast Asian nations are actively competing for dominance in the digital economy. Indonesia is prioritizing tighter resource control and infrastructure spending under President Prabowo Subianto, while Vietnam is upgrading its seaport network to strengthen its position as a global manufacturing alternative to China. At the same time, East Asian economies like Japan and South Korea are actively recruiting tech talent from Southeast Asia to address their own domestic shortages.

Singapore is deepening physical and economic ties with Malaysia while navigating intense regional competition for supply chain dominance and technical talent.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The geopolitical and technological rivalry between the United States and China is directly impacting Singapore. The United States has flagged Singapore as one of several economies potentially used for "shadow transshipment" to evade tariffs on Chinese goods, forcing local authorities to tighten export controls. Furthermore, Washington is expected to pressure international partners to align with its position in the global artificial intelligence race, complicating Singapore's neutral diplomatic stance.

Despite these pressures, Singapore remains a highly attractive destination for global technology capital. **OpenAI** is in talks to lease a massive office space in Shaw Tower, and **Anthropic** has secured a physical presence in the financial district. This influx of foreign tech firms keeps commercial real estate demand high, but it also accelerates the local demand for specialized talent and energy resources.
