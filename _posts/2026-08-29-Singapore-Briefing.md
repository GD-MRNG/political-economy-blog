## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singaporeans are experiencing a dual reality of rising daily costs and expanded state support. While core inflation remains sticky due to surging electricity prices and high private transport costs, the government has introduced a major policy reset to help families. The **National Day Rally 2026** announced significant increases in childcare leave, higher income ceilings for public housing, and direct financial payouts. 

At the same time, the workplace is undergoing structural shifts. Retrenchments are rising due to corporate restructuring, even as overall employment grows. Meanwhile, regional environmental risks are returning, with Indonesian forest fires prompting local haze preparations and health advisories.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost stack for residents is growing heavier in specific areas, particularly energy and healthcare. **Core inflation** reached 2% in July, driven primarily by surging electricity prices. In the private transport market, **Certificate of Entitlement (COE)** premiums continue to surge despite quiet showrooms. Healthcare costs are also rising, with patients facing larger out-of-pocket expenses and rising premiums for **Integrated Shield Plan (IP)** riders.

To cushion these pressures, the government distributed $500 in **CDC Vouchers** to households. Civil servants received salary adjustments of 2% to 9% to match market rates, alongside mid-year bonuses. In the property market, the government raised the income ceiling for **Build-To-Order (BTO)** flats to help more families secure public housing, though private resale prices showed a slight dip of 0.3% in July. The core dynamic is a tightly managed cost of living where targeted state subsidies are deployed to offset rising utility and healthcare expenses.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market presents a mixed outlook of targeted tech opportunities and restructuring-driven layoffs. While overall employment continues to expand, retrenchments rose due to restructuring in outward-oriented sectors. **GovTech** is undergoing layoffs as part of its restructuring, though the agency aims to redeploy affected staff. In contrast, the financial and tech sectors continue to hire, with 2,000 curated tech roles made available for fresh graduates and upskillers.

Workplace culture and employee wellness are emerging as critical issues. A national survey revealed that 61% of Singapore workers report burnout, with pre-school teachers citing heavy workloads and parental expectations as primary stressors. The expansion of mandatory childcare leave has also raised concerns among small and medium enterprises (SMEs) regarding manpower shortages and team workloads. The core dynamic is a transition toward high-value tech roles amid rising restructuring layoffs and widespread workplace burnout.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is steering Singapore toward long-term sustainability, digital resilience, and stronger social safety nets. Under the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise 2030 (RIE2030)** plan, the government is investing S$800 million in transport research and has introduced **SS 726**, a new standard for tropical data centre liquid cooling to manage the energy demands of the AI boom. The government is also tightening regulations on data centres, with potential license revocation for operators that fail to meet strict resilience rules.

On the social front, the government is rolling out the **SG Child Support Package** to provide cash benefits to children of unwed parents and expanding **Community Chest** funding for mental health projects. To address community friction, the **Community Disputes Resolution Unit** pilot for severe noise and hoarding will expand nationwide. The core direction is the heavy regulation of digital infrastructure paired with expanded social infrastructure to support families and caregivers.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic integration with its immediate neighbours while managing shared environmental challenges. Planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a potential launch in March. This zone is expected to help both countries weather global tariff shifts, though analysts note a stark automation gap, with Singapore using 770 robots per 10,000 workers compared to Johor's 215.

Bilateral transport links are expanding, with the **Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** progress and new direct flights connecting Singapore to secondary Indonesian cities like Belitung, Padang, and Pontianak. However, regional environmental risks are rising as Indonesia battles severe forest fires in West Kalimantan, threatening to bring toxic haze back to Singapore. The core dynamic is a push for deeper economic and physical connectivity with Malaysia and Indonesia, balanced against recurring transboundary environmental threats.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions and the AI spending boom are directly impacting Singapore's position as a trusted business hub. The US government has flagged 40 economies, including Singapore, as part of China's shadow transshipment network, and is currently probing Singapore-based **Apex Logistics** over the alleged smuggling of restricted Nvidia chips. This scrutiny forces Singapore to balance its trade relationships carefully, as the government warns that Singapore must maintain a hard-to-replicate edge in global supply chains.

Meanwhile, the global AI boom continues to channel massive capital into Singapore. The government has allocated 200MW of new data centre capacity to four operators, even as global tech giants like **OpenAI** expand their regional presence by hiring executives to lead Southeast Asian operations from Singapore. This influx of tech investment keeps Singapore at the centre of the regional digital economy but places unprecedented strain on the local power grid.
