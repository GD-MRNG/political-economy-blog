## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singapore is experiencing a stark divergence between its macroeconomic success and the daily reality of its residents. While the **Straits Times Index (STI)** is rising and local banks are posting record profits driven by a global wealth boom, the cost of living and job security are pressing heavily on working professionals. A striking 60% of Singaporeans now report holding a second job to cope with financial demands. 

At the same time, the physical and economic boundaries of the country are shifting. The upcoming **Johor Bahru-Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** will soon reduce travel time to Malaysia to just five minutes, forcing local merchants to brace for intense competition. Meanwhile, corporate restructuring is driving up retrenchments, leaving even highly qualified local professionals facing longer job searches.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost stack for households remains heavy, even as core inflation stabilized at 1.6% in June. Daily expenses are rising, and the food and beverage sector is feeling the squeeze, marked by the high-profile closures of long-standing establishments like **Fika Swedish Cafe and Bistro**, **Pantler**, and the **Tiger Beer** brewery in Jurong. To cushion this pressure, the government has deployed targeted relief, including $500 in **CDC Vouchers** and price freezes on 500 daily essentials by **FairPrice Group**.

Housing remains a primary source of stress for younger professionals. Traditional homeownership is becoming harder to reach, prompting over 500 young Singaporeans to apply for a new government co-living pilot scheme that was five times oversubscribed. In response, the government is actively studying more rental housing options to meet this growing demand for independent living. 

To protect the economy from external shocks, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** is allowing the Singapore Dollar to strengthen. This policy helps shield the country from import inflation, especially as global energy markets remain volatile due to conflicts in the Middle East. 

*Core dynamic: Targeted state subsidies are temporarily cushioning households against high structural costs in housing, food, and energy.*

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market is undergoing a structural shift. While overall employment is growing, retrenchments have risen to their highest levels since 2020, driven largely by corporate restructuring in outward-oriented sectors. Even the public sector is not immune, with **GovTech** cutting 93 roles as part of an effort to streamline digital services. 

This restructuring has left 3,600 fresh graduates still searching for work, prompting the government to extend graduate traineeship programmes. Meanwhile, competition at the top remains intense. The number of global talent visa (**One Pass**) holders grew to 8,500, with the majority of these high-earning professionals concentrated in the financial and insurance sectors. 

Technology is also changing the terms of employment. While companies are eager to adopt artificial intelligence, many are struggling to scale these tools effectively due to fragmented data systems. For workers, this transition has brought new anxieties, with employment lawyers reporting a rise in "disguised retrenchments" and companies requiring workers to sign non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) to secure their redundancy payouts.

*Core dynamic: A restructuring job market is squeezing mid-level and entry-level professionals while the threshold for specialized global talent rises.*

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant funding and regulatory energy toward long-term national resilience. In a major organizational shift, the Ministry of Trade and Industry is being renamed the **Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry**, signaling a massive policy pivot toward securing the nation's power supply. This is accompanied by a S$800 million investment in transport research and the conditional approval to import 900 MW of solar power from Malaysia.

Addressing the demographic crisis is another urgent priority. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong has announced a major review of family support measures, which will include enhanced childcare subsidies and expanded maternity leave to ease the financial burden of raising children. 

The state is also preparing the workforce for an AI-driven future from the ground up. The **Ministry of Education (MOE)** is mandating that all primary, secondary, and junior college teachers complete compulsory AI training modules. For workers displaced by automation, the government has introduced transition packages, such as training incentives of up to S$1,600 for point-to-point drivers transitioning to autonomous vehicle operations.

*Core direction: The state is aggressively underwriting energy security, AI integration, and family welfare to preserve long-term national competitiveness.*

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its physical and economic integration with its immediate neighbors. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a formal launch expected in March. This zone, combined with the five-minute commute promised by the RTS Link, will make it much easier for Singapore-based businesses to locate back-office operations and manufacturing in Johor to escape high domestic costs.

However, this integration presents a severe challenge to local heartland merchants. Neighborhood businesses are already reporting financial strain from e-commerce platforms like Taobao and Pinduoduo, and they now face the prospect of local consumers redirecting their weekend spending to Malaysia. 

To secure its green transition, Singapore is leveraging regional resources rather than relying solely on domestic generation. The approval of solar power imports from Malaysia, including facilities at the **Linggiu Reservoir**, shows that Singapore's utility grid is becoming increasingly transnational.

*Core dynamic: Deepening physical and economic integration with Johor is redefining Singapore's borders, offering resource relief but threatening local retail.*

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions are directly affecting Singapore's industrial and financial sectors. Ongoing maritime conflicts in the Strait of Hormuz have kept global oil prices high, which will eventually feed into local electricity bills and increase operating costs for Singapore's manufacturing plants. 

US-China trade friction also continues to ripple through local technology supply chains. While US courts have temporarily paused the blacklisting of Chinese tech firms like **WuXi AppTec**, local semiconductor and electronics manufacturers are operating under constant tariff pressure. Despite these headwinds, Singapore's factory output grew by 7.2% in June, buoyed by the global demand for AI-related hardware.
