## THE GROUND PICTURE

Living and working in Singapore right now means navigating a high-cost environment where the government is actively trying to buffer citizens from global pressures while restructuring the economy for an AI-driven future. The material reality is defined by a heavy cost stack, particularly in housing and transport, alongside rising corporate restructuring that is pushing retrenchments to their highest quarterly levels since 2020. While the economy is projected to grow by 4.5% to 5.5% in 2026, the average professional feels a disconnect between these macroeconomic numbers and their daily financial security.

At the same time, the physical environment is facing seasonal and structural strains. Regional wildfires in Kalimantan and East Kalimantan are triggering transboundary haze alerts, prompting local adjustments such as shortened Friday sermons and public health warnings. The government is preparing for these environmental disruptions while simultaneously managing long-term infrastructure upgrades, such as aging MRT networks and new transit lines, which are causing localized noise and disruptions.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost stack for Singapore residents remains heavy, driven by persistent increases in daily essentials, transport, and housing. **COE premiums** have risen across most categories, with Category A prices reaching $128,501, while **YouTube Premium** and other digital services have implemented double-digit price hikes. To mitigate these cost-of-living pressures, the government brought forward the distribution of **CDC vouchers** to June 2026 and **FairPrice Group** froze prices on over 500 daily essentials. However, long-term financial anxiety remains high, with studies showing that a significant majority of adults worry about outliving their savings, especially when facing potential critical illness recovery costs that can reach nearly $170,000.

The housing market presents a mixed picture of high costs and shifting demand. Non-landed private residential rents rose 1.6% in July, hitting a new monthly volume high, and condo rents reached a 12-month peak. Conversely, the public housing market is seeing adjustments; the costs of public **Community Care Apartments** were reduced and basic services cut due to lackluster demand, while two-thirds of **Sentosa Cove** properties resold at a loss. Meanwhile, younger Singaporeans are increasingly exploring renting homes abroad in cities like Bangkok to escape high domestic costs, even if they already own property locally. 

The core dynamic at work is a highly pressurized domestic cost structure that is forcing both citizens and businesses to seek cross-border relief.

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

Singapore’s job market is expanding, but this growth is accompanied by rising retrenchments driven by corporate restructuring in outward-oriented sectors. **GovTech** is among the organizations undergoing layoffs as part of plans to streamline digital services, though the government emphasizes these cuts are due to restructuring rather than direct AI replacement. For professionals in the technology and services sectors, the hiring landscape is shifting toward a preference for practical work experience over paper qualifications. However, local tech workers face intense competition, and those in outsourced roles are vulnerable as regional peers in India and the Philippines rapidly adapt to AI tools.

Within specific industries, talent strain and operational pressures are mounting. The semiconductor sector, represented by firms like **UMC**, is flagging rising costs and talent shortages at its Singapore fabs. In the retail and food and beverage sectors, nine in ten firms report hiring strains, with a majority of workers actively seeking better pay. To address these challenges, the **Jobs-Skills Integrator for the Retail Industry** has been introduced to support workforce development, and the **Progressive Wage Model** is being expanded to guarantee outsourced workers a minimum of ten days of annual leave starting in 2029. 

The core dynamic is a restructuring job market where employment growth is offset by targeted layoffs and a widening skills gap in high-value tech roles.

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The Singapore government is directing significant regulatory energy and capital toward digital safety, family support, and green infrastructure. In preparation for the **National Day Rally 2026**, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong has signaled that family support, parenthood, and the evolving social contract will be central policy themes. On the digital front, the **Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)** and the police are tightening controls on online scams, introducing a new code requiring social media platforms to verify advertisers, and beta-testing a new AI chatbot to make official information more accessible. Furthermore, the **Ministry of Home Affairs** has proposed granting police the power to order banks and telcos to disable scam-linked accounts.

In terms of industrial policy, the state is heavily backing deep-tech and green energy capabilities. The government is investing $800 million in transport research and innovation under the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise 2030 (RIE2030)** plan, which includes testing driverless buses and self-driving trucks at the port. To meet surging power demands from the digital economy, **Tuas Power** and **PacificLight** are constructing 670-MW hydrogen-ready gas turbine plants. Additionally, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** has introduced new measures to strengthen Singapore’s position as a global asset management hub, successfully pausing fund manager relocations to Hong Kong.

The core direction is a dual focus on tightening digital and financial regulations while funding large-scale green energy and deep-tech infrastructure.

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic integration with its immediate neighbors while managing competitive pressures. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, and Johor has already netted over $1.2 billion in potential investment bids following bilateral discussions. The **Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** is nearing completion, with station and rail infrastructure in Malaysia finalized ahead of its opening next year. While this link promises seamless travel, Singaporean retailers and food and beverage businesses are expressing concern over increased cost competition as Johor Bahru becomes a five-minute train ride away.

Beyond Malaysia, Singapore is expanding its strategic partnerships across ASEAN and Central Asia. The first **Singapore-Vietnam Strategic Dialogue** focused on talent development and technology cooperation, aligning with Vietnam's efforts to upgrade its maritime and digital infrastructure. However, regional environmental challenges persist, as unhealthy air quality in Malaysia and Indonesia has prompted calls for collective ASEAN action to address transboundary haze. Additionally, delays in the **ASEAN Power Grid** project are estimated to cost the region up to $2.6 billion annually, highlighting the infrastructure bottlenecks that Singapore must help navigate to secure its future energy imports.

The core dynamic is an accelerating economic integration with Johor that offers massive investment opportunities but threatens local retail and service margins.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions and supply chain shifts are directly impacting Singapore's open economy. The United States has flagged forty economies, including Singapore, as part of China’s shadow transshipment network, increasing compliance and scrutiny for local logistics and trading firms. This comes alongside investigations by US and Singapore authorities into major local iron ore traders like **Radiant World**. Furthermore, US President Donald Trump’s threats of economic tariffs on China and its trading partners are driving up global oil prices and causing volatility in bond markets, which directly feeds into Singapore's domestic inflation and energy costs.

The global AI boom is also reshaping local corporate costs. **Nvidia** has notified customers of AI-related price hikes exceeding 15% for systems shipping in early 2027, which will raise capital expenditure for Singapore's expanding data centers. To manage these resource demands, Singapore is increasing regulatory pressure on data centers, warning operators that they face license revocation if they fail to meet strict energy resilience and green standards. This regulatory tightening is occurring just as global tech giants like **Alibaba** place multi-billion dollar share offerings in Hong Kong to fund their global AI expansions, signaling that the regional race for digital infrastructure dominance is accelerating.
