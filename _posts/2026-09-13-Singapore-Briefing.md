## THE GROUND PICTURE

Living and working in Singapore right now means navigating a sharp contrast between a booming financial sector and tightening daily expenses. The local stock market is experiencing its best performance in years, and major local banks like **OCBC** and **DBS** are being recognized as top global performers. Yet, on the ground, residents are feeling the squeeze of high fuel prices, record-breaking car ownership costs, and rising food prices. 

At the same time, physical and environmental frictions are returning to daily life. The haze has returned to Singapore, with air quality occasionally dipping into the unhealthy range and prompting the **National Environment Agency (NEA)** to issue daily advisories. Major infrastructure projects are also hitting physical limits, with the **Land Transport Authority (LTA)** reporting that construction of the **North-South Corridor** has entered its most difficult phase due to hard rock and water seepage.

For professionals in the technology and corporate sectors, the environment is highly volatile. While Singapore is attracting massive data center investments, local businesses are struggling with high operating costs. Well-known brands like True Fitness and the Baker’s Brew bakery chain have suddenly gone into liquidation, leaving employees unpaid and consumers holding losses. 

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost of living remains the primary concern for the vast majority of Singaporeans. Although real wages rose slightly last year due to moderating inflation, core inflation remains a pressure point. The cost stack is getting heavier, driven by high utility bills, expensive fuel, and a record-high Category A Certificate of Entitlement (COE) price of $133,009. To help lower-income households, commercial operators like **FairPrice** have doubled their discounts for selected cardholders, but middle-class budgets remain tight.

The retail and food sectors are undergoing a visible restructuring. While high-end international brands like Thailand's Gentlewoman are opening flagship stores on Orchard Road, mid-tier food and beverage outlets are closing down. The **Crystal Jade** restaurant chain closed its Great World outlet, marking its third closure this year. Analysts warn that shopping malls are raising rents to recover their own costs, which is squeezing retail tenants and limiting returns for property investors.

In the financial space, the strong Singapore dollar and high global interest rates are keeping mortgage rates elevated. While younger generations are increasingly choosing to invest in stocks rather than buying property, those looking for homes face high prices. The private residential market remains expensive, with new projects like Amberwood at Holland Plain launching at high per-square-foot prices, though the government is building new public housing flats with shorter waiting times in Toa Payoh and Sin Ming.

*The core dynamic at work is that high asset prices and global supply pressures are keeping the local cost stack heavy despite wage adjustments.*

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market is highly divided. In the technology sector, hiring remains active for roles tied to artificial intelligence and data infrastructure. However, global tech giants are cutting costs elsewhere. **Oracle** is spending an additional US$700 million on global job cuts to manage the massive cash requirements of building AI data centers, a trend that affects regional headcounts. Local semiconductor fabrication plants, such as those run by **UMC** in Singapore, are also reporting severe talent strain and rising operational costs.

Outside of tech, employment security is mixed. The sudden closure of True Fitness and True Yoga left over 200 staff and freelancers without jobs or Central Provident Fund (CPF) contributions for September. The **Singapore Fitness Alliance** has stepped in to help these displaced workers find new employment. Meanwhile, the hospitality and entry-level sectors are seeing a hiring surge, but overall corporate hiring remains highly selective as companies prioritize productivity over headcount.

For platform and delivery workers, the government is introducing stronger safety nets. The **National Delivery Champions Association** has been formally recognized by Foodpanda to represent workers on welfare issues. Additionally, the government has launched defensive riding workshops to train 1,400 riders, following a doubling of platform worker deaths in the first half of the year. However, industry reports show that platform workers still face a significant healthcare gap and lack routine medical care.

*The core dynamic is that AI-driven demand is creating highly specialized tech roles while squeezing traditional operations and mid-tier service jobs.*

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is focusing its policy energy on family support, digital security, and public sector leadership. At the **National Day Rally 2026**, the government announced that working parents will receive up to 12 days of fully funded childcare leave, alongside a $500 credit via the **LifeSG** platform. These measures are part of a refreshed social compact aimed at encouraging couples to have children despite high living costs.

To support local businesses, the government launched the **BizSG Portal** to streamline how small and medium enterprises access state support. In the digital space, new laws have been passed that grant the police powers to order banks and telecommunications companies to immediately block scam accounts. This comes as Singapore continues to fight cross-border syndicates, including a Cambodia-based scam that cost Singaporeans over $52 million.

Public sector salaries have also been adjusted to keep pace with the private market. Political office holders received a pay increase of up to 9 percent, bringing ministerial salaries to $1.2 million, while Member of Parliament allowances rose to $18,500 monthly. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong defended these increases, stating that competitive pay is necessary to attract capable leaders and maintain high civil service standards without compromising public trust.

*The state is actively subsidizing family formation and tightening digital security while adjusting public sector wages to secure administrative talent.*

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore’s economic relationship with its neighbors is entering a highly integrated phase. Planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a launch expected in March. This integration is accelerated by the upcoming **Johor Bahru-Singapore RTS Link** train service, scheduled to open in January 2027. The ease of travel is already prompting Singapore merchants to reassess their competitiveness, as local shoppers increasingly head to Johor for retail and food.

This cross-border flow is also changing healthcare planning. The Ministry of Health is currently reviewing the **Overseas MediSave** scheme to allow Singaporeans to use their medical savings for treatments in Johor once the RTS Link opens. However, integration also brings shared challenges. Johor is rapidly expanding its data center capacity, which competes with Singapore on scale, while Malaysian food markets are warning of local price increases driven by the influx of Singaporean shoppers.

Environmental and political instability in the region remains a constant background risk. Forest fires in Indonesia have triggered regional haze, causing a spike in respiratory illnesses across Southeast Asia. Politically, Malaysia's ruling coalition faces internal tensions, with the leadership of UMNO challenging Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim over the timing of the next general election, which could impact bilateral policy continuity.

*Deepening integration with Johor is reshaping Singapore's retail and healthcare boundaries while exposing the city-state to regional environmental and resource pressures.*

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global competition for artificial intelligence infrastructure is directly impacting Singapore's physical supply chains. The massive build-out of AI data centers worldwide has created a shortage of computer components, causing PC prices in Singapore to surge by up to 40 percent. This hardware inflation is raising the cost of technology upgrades for local businesses. To fund these capital-intensive data centers, infrastructure firms are raising massive debt locally, as seen by Blackstone’s AirTrunk seeking a S$2 billion loan for its Singapore IPO.

Geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East are also hitting home through energy costs. Houthi rebel attacks on shipping in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz have forced the closure of vital pipelines and restricted tanker travel. These disruptions have driven global oil prices higher, keeping local petrol prices elevated and raising electricity costs for Singaporean households. Additionally, global central banks are facing pressure to raise interest rates further following high inflation data from the United States, which will keep local borrowing costs high for the foreseeable future.
