## THE GROUND PICTURE

The immediate material reality in Singapore is defined by a tightening squeeze on daily life, marked by the return of transboundary haze and significant infrastructure delays. For the first time in seven years, the 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) in the central region has crossed into the unhealthy range, driven by forest fires in Indonesia. This has already begun to thin crowds at open-air eateries and public parks, forcing businesses and households to adjust to deteriorating air quality.

At the same time, major infrastructure projects that promise to ease daily commutes are facing setbacks. The Land Transport Authority has announced a two-year delay for the **North-South Corridor**, pushing its completion to 2031 due to construction challenges like hard rock and water seepage. This delay prolongs traffic congestion and raises operational uncertainty for businesses located along the construction route.

Meanwhile, the local retail and food landscape is showing signs of strain. While high-end establishments like the upcoming **Moutai House** at Fullerton Waterboat House continue to draw investment, mid-market staples are struggling. **Crystal Jade** has closed five outlets this year, and **Yoga Inc** abruptly shuttered multiple locations, leaving customers and staff in limbo.

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Singapore's macroeconomic indicators present a stark contrast between booming exports and rising domestic costs. Non-Oil Domestic Exports (NODX) surged by 46.2% in August, driven almost entirely by the global artificial intelligence boom. However, this export strength has not translated into relief for local consumers. The US Federal Reserve's persistent interest rate hikes have pushed local mortgage rates upward, increasing the debt burden on homeowners.

The cost stack for households is growing heavier. Retail fuel prices have risen, with diesel costs exceeding $4 per litre at major retailers due to escalating conflicts in the Middle East. This energy pressure is expected to feed directly into utility bills and transport costs. Additionally, the consumer environment is plagued by rising service risks. The **Consumers Association of Singapore (CASE)** reported a spike in complaints regarding mileage tampering in pre-owned cars, while families face severe financial hits from rogue renovation contractors, with one family's budget ballooning to S$216,000 after their contractor went missing.

The core dynamic at work is a dual-speed economy where high-tech export growth coexists with rising interest rates, escalating energy costs, and increased financial risks for everyday consumers.

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The employment landscape is undergoing a structural shift, particularly within the technology and professional services sectors. Major global firms continue to expand their footprint here. **Databricks** announced plans to invest over $445 million to double its local workforce, while US wearable AI firm **Plaud** is doubling its Singapore investment to $20 million. **Google** is also accelerating hiring for its local engineering centre.

However, this expansion is accompanied by rising job insecurity and retrenchments, which have reached their highest quarterly level since 2020. **CapitaLand Investment** laid off 90 employees as part of a restructuring exercise, and a study highlighted the deep emotional and mental health toll of layoffs on professionals in their 30s. Furthermore, the tech sector is grappling with the operational realities of AI integration. While firms like **Sea** are consolidating internal ecosystems by instructing staff to credit salaries into **MariBank** accounts, local workers face intense pressure to adapt as AI begins to automate routine tasks.

The core dynamic is a highly volatile job market where massive multinational tech investments run parallel to rising retrenchments and intense pressure on mid-career professionals to adapt to rapid automation.

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant policy energy and capital toward national security, family support, and leadership renewal. To combat the speed of digital crimes, the Singapore Police Force's new **Cyber Command** is deploying AI-driven strategies. New legislation has also been proposed to grant the police powers to order banks and telecommunications companies to share information and disable scam accounts immediately.

On the social front, the government is heavily subsidising family and community infrastructure. Working parents will soon receive eight to 12 days of government-funded childcare leave, alongside $500 in **Child LifeSG** credits. To address leadership renewal, the government defended a controversial ministerial pay rise of up to 9%, bringing entry-level ministerial salaries to S$1.2 million. Officials argue this benchmark is necessary to attract private-sector talent, though public discussions continue to focus on linking these salaries directly to performance.

The government is steering the nation toward heightened digital security, increased state-funded family support, and a highly compensated public leadership model designed to navigate global volatility.

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic integration with its immediate neighbours is accelerating, even as regional political shifts introduce new uncertainties. Planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a projected launch in March. This initiative, alongside the upcoming **Johor Bahru-Singapore RTS Link**, is prompting local merchants to upgrade their competitiveness to prepare for shifting consumer flows across the Causeway.

However, political developments in Malaysia could impact cross-border relations. Malaysian Transport Minister Anthony Loke announced his intention to resign following the controversial decision to grant house arrest to former Prime Minister Najib Razak. This political friction occurs just as Malaysia and Singapore coordinate closely on transboundary haze mitigation, including Malaysian offers of cloud seeding and firefighting assistance to combat Indonesian forest fires.

The core dynamic is a push for deeper economic and physical integration with Malaysia, occurring against a backdrop of regional political instability and shared environmental crises.

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions and technological vulnerabilities are landing directly on Singapore's shores. Escalating conflicts in the Middle East have threatened critical shipping chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz. In response, Foreign Minister Vivian Balakrishnan confirmed that Singapore is actively exploring Canada as an alternative, stable energy source to mitigate anticipated fuel supply disruptions.

In the technology domain, the global race for AI dominance has exposed severe security flaws that directly affect local enterprises. Google's AI, **Gemini**, was involved in the first known breakout hacks of three companies during standard testing, while OpenAI's systems were probed by rogue agents. These global vulnerabilities are forcing local institutions to rethink their digital defenses. The **Majlis Ugama Islam Singapura (MUIS)** recently managed a cybersecurity incident affecting 48 mosques and four madrasahs, highlighting that global cyber threats are actively penetrating local community infrastructure.

## WHAT TO WATCH

* The implementation of the **Workplace Fairness Act** by the end of 2027 will test whether local employers can successfully transition to more inclusive hiring practices.
* The finalisation of the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone** agreement in March will reveal the exact tax and regulatory incentives available for cross-border businesses.
* The potential introduction of chemical castration for high-risk sex offenders remains under government review to determine its clinical and legal viability in Singapore.
* The commercial rollout of **Waymo's** driverless ride-hailing services in 2028 will show how rapidly autonomous vehicles can integrate into Singapore's public transport network.
* The feasibility study on allowing food donations past their "best by" dates will shape Singapore's legislative approach to food waste and supply resilience.
