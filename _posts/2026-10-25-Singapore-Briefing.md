## THE GROUND PICTURE

The physical and environmental conditions in Singapore have grown more challenging. Transboundary haze from Indonesian forest fires has returned, pushing air quality into the unhealthy range and prompting the **National Environment Agency (NEA)** to issue legal notices to Singapore-based firms. 

At the same time, Indonesia has cut its natural gas exports to Singapore to prioritize its own domestic market. While the **Energy Market Authority (EMA)** has assured the public that these cuts represent less than two percent of daily demand, they highlight Singapore's vulnerability to regional resource decisions.

These environmental and resource pressures are landing on a population already dealing with persistent cost-of-living concerns. The government has pledged to focus its current term on tackling these rising daily costs and securing better jobs for citizens.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Core inflation eased to two percent in September, but the cost stack remains heavy for residents. Public transport fares are rising, and spot electricity prices have hit a 2026 high. Petrol prices also rose four times in a single week across major providers, including **Caltex**, **Esso**, **Shell**, and **Sinopec**. 

In the property market, private home prices rose 1.4 percent in the third quarter, even as transaction volumes slowed. Certificate of Entitlement (COE) premiums for Category A and B both exceeded $131,000 in the latest tender. To buffer against these persistent price pressures, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** is maintaining the appreciation rate of the Singapore Dollar.

The core economic dynamic at work is that rising utility, transport, and housing costs are squeezing household budgets despite easing core inflation.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The employment landscape is experiencing structural shifts, particularly in the technology sector. **GovTech** conducted a second wave of layoffs, retrenching 38 employees, while gaming giant **Ubisoft** cut 70 roles in Singapore. To protect entry-level positions from AI disruption, the government is offering year-long internships to 3,000 tertiary students. 

Meanwhile, the **National Wages Council (NWC)** has recommended a six to eight percent salary increase for lower-wage workers. Bus captain starting salaries will also rise by $450 from 2027 under a new tripartite agreement. For gig workers, **Foodpanda** has introduced full-time delivery roles offering an $1,800 base salary and full **Central Provident Fund (CPF)** contributions.

The core employment dynamic is a bifurcated job market where tech firms are restructuring and automating, while the state and unions push for wage increases and AI-reskilling safeguards for lower-income and entry-level workers.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is focusing its regulatory and financial power on digital infrastructure and public safety. Parliament passed a new law requiring data centres with a critical IT load of at least three megawatts to be licensed. **Enterprise Singapore (EnterpriseSG)** is investing $45 million to help manufacturing small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) adopt AI tools. 

To combat digital threats, the **Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA)** audited major app stores and removed 65 harmful apps. The **CPF Board** has launched the "Activate your safeguards" initiative to protect retirement savings from digital scams. Additionally, the government is running trials for AI agents, with over 12,000 AI agents already deployed in public healthcare.

The core government direction is the aggressive regulation of the digital space and data infrastructure while funding AI adoption to drive economic productivity.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Bilateral relations with Indonesia are strained by energy cuts and environmental issues. Indonesia's reduction of natural gas exports cuts about eight percent of the pipeline gas Singapore imported in 2024. In response to the haze, the **NEA** issued legal notices to ten Singapore-based companies, including **Asia Pulp & Paper** and **Wilmar**, to investigate their links to Indonesian forest fires. 

On a positive note, economic integration with Malaysia is deepening. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion for a potential March launch. Furthermore, **Bank Negara Malaysia** and the **Bank of Thailand** have joined the **MAS** BLOOM initiative to align cross-border digital payment systems.

The core regional dynamic is that Singapore is navigating resource and environmental pressures from Indonesia while deepening financial and economic integration with Malaysia.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global doubts over debt-fueled AI expansion have caused international tech stocks to plunge, with **Goldman Sachs** cutting its target for South Korea's Kospi index. Despite this global market volatility, local demand for AI infrastructure remains robust. 

**GIC** is backing a $1 billion initial public offering for an **NTT** data centre real estate investment trust. AI-related demand also continues to drive up rents and prices for Singapore's industrial spaces, which saw a 4.2 percent price increase in the third quarter.
