## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singaporeans are feeling a squeeze in their daily lives as the cost of basic necessities rises alongside environmental and economic pressures. Air quality has periodically dipped into the unhealthy range due to regional haze, forcing residents to rely on air purifiers and monitor daily pollution indexes. At the same time, daily commuting is becoming more expensive, with public transport fares set to rise and petrol prices climbing.

While the broader economy shows growth, the local job market is quietly cooling. Retrenchments are up, and finding a new role is taking longer for displaced workers. Meanwhile, the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence is starting to show its double-edged nature, bringing both operational efficiency and new risks like Singapore's first reported AI-related data breach.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost stack for households is growing heavier. Core inflation rose to 2.2 percent in August, driven by persistent price pressures. Public transport fares will increase by up to 13 cents for adults in December, and mortgage rates are climbing following global interest rate hikes. Petrol prices have also seen consecutive daily increases, adding to the cost of driving.

Food and dining costs are highly volatile. While major retailers like **FairPrice Group** are holding prices on staples like eggs and frozen chicken, the food and beverage sector is struggling. High rents and rising operating costs have forced several popular eateries and patisseries to close their doors. These pressures are causing citizens to question the adequacy of retirement safety nets like the **CPF LIFE** annuity scheme.

In the property market, private home prices continue to rise, driven by landed properties, even as sales volumes drop. The government has adjusted policies for Executive Condominiums by raising the income ceiling, which drew a record bid for a site in Canberra Drive. However, buyers of new private launches are facing slimmer resale gains due to high initial purchase prices.

**The core dynamic at work is that rising structural costs in transport, housing, and energy are outpacing wage growth and squeezing household budgets.**

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

Singapore’s labor market expanded in the second quarter, but this growth masks a cooling trend. Retrenchments rose to 4,620, and job vacancies are falling. Displaced workers are finding it harder to secure new employment quickly, and employers are increasingly redirecting their salary budgets toward highly scarce technical skills rather than general hiring.

The technology sector is navigating a complex transition. Companies are eager to deploy AI to boost productivity, but this has introduced significant operational and security challenges. The data breach at **Bee Cheng Hiang**, caused by an employee using AI-generated code, highlights the lack of oversight in rapid tech adoption. Furthermore, nearly all IT leaders report that AI tools have accessed sensitive corporate data without permission, prompting firms to rethink their cybersecurity strategies.

Traditional sectors are also adjusting to these shifts. **SPH Media** is restructuring under new leadership to integrate AI into its operations, while the maritime and transport sectors are managing to weather market declines due to strong export surges. However, platform workers, such as private-hire drivers and delivery riders, face growing pressure to take physical risks as their earnings are squeezed, despite new legal protections.

**The core dynamic is that the job market is shifting from broad-based hiring to highly selective recruitment focused on specialized skills, leaving general workers and platform riders more vulnerable.**

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant funding and regulatory attention toward social support and family infrastructure. Under the **National Day Rally 2026** initiatives, working parents will receive up to 12 days of fully funded childcare leave and a new **Child LifeSG** credit of 500 dollars. The government is also increasing its spending on persons with disabilities by up to 60 percent over the next five years, which includes dropping care fees at Adult Disability Homes by up to 80 percent by 2028.

To support this inclusive push, three polyclinics will introduce tailored services for persons with intellectual disabilities and autism by 2028. **SG Enable** is also launching a one-stop center to help disabled individuals find employment and assist companies with job redesign. Meanwhile, the **People's Association** is training volunteers to identify and support isolated seniors living alone.

On the regulatory front, the government is tightening rules around digital safety and emerging technologies. New laws grant the police stronger powers to order banks and telecommunications companies to block scam accounts. The **Infocomm Media Development Authority** is also introducing licensing regimes to improve the security and sustainability of data centers, while the **Central Provident Fund Board** is actively educating the public on retirement readiness and nomination procedures.

**The core direction is that the state is expanding social safety nets for families and vulnerable citizens while tightening regulatory controls over digital platforms, scams, and data infrastructure.**

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic ties with its immediate neighbors are deepening, but this brings new competitive dynamics. The **Johor Bahru-Singapore RTS Link** is nearing completion after years of delay, with passenger service now targeted for February 2027. While this link will improve connectivity, it is expected to put pressure on Singaporean grocers and food operators, as local consumers could spend over one billion dollars more annually in Johor.

Plans for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** are nearing completion, with a formal launch expected soon. This zone is designed to attract third-country investors and serve as an ASEAN gateway for Chinese businesses. However, Malaysia and Thailand are also aggressively building their own capabilities, with Thailand attracting a major semiconductor complex from Infineon Technologies, challenging Singapore's dominance in high-tech manufacturing.

Environmental and resource challenges remain a shared regional risk. Indonesia is reviving its peatland and mangrove agency to combat the forest fires that cause transboundary haze, but Singapore still faces periodic air quality issues. Additionally, low water levels in Indonesian rivers due to dry weather have disrupted coal and palm oil shipments, highlighting how climate volatility in the region directly impacts supply chains.

**The core dynamic is that deepening physical and economic integration with Malaysia offers new growth opportunities but threatens local retail and food businesses with intense cross-border price competition.**

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions are directly impacting Singapore's open economy. The ongoing conflict in the Middle East has forced **Singapore Airlines** and **Scoot** to cancel or reroute flights, while volatile global energy prices are driving up local utility and transport costs. Furthermore, the threat of reciprocal tariffs between the US, the EU, and China is forcing multinational corporations to restructure their supply chains, with some looking to Singapore as a stable neutral hub.

The global race for AI supremacy is also landing locally. As US tech giants and Chinese firms like DeepSeek advance their AI capabilities, Singapore is experiencing a massive influx of data center investments. However, this boom is running into local land and power constraints, forcing the government to prioritize energy efficiency and explore alternative energy sources like nuclear fusion.

**The core dynamic is that global trade conflicts and energy shocks are driving up local operating costs while cementing Singapore's role as a critical, albeit resource-constrained, safe haven for global technology and capital.**
