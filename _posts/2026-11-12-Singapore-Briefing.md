## THE GROUND PICTURE

Working professionals in Singapore are navigating a dual reality of rising domestic costs and escalating global security risks. While daily life is shaped by the persistent inflation of transport, food, and housing, the workplace is increasingly exposed to international geopolitical friction. 

The sudden resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang amid a scandal has sent shockwaves through the local technology sector. A Singapore-based firm, **Megaspeed**, is under intense scrutiny for allegedly helping Chinese entities bypass US chip export curbs. This development, coupled with a surge in local data breaches affecting organizations like **SPH Media** and the **Agency for Integrated Care (AIC)**, highlights how vulnerable Singapore is to global digital conflicts.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The domestic cost stack remains heavy for the average household. Singapore’s interbank interest rate has reached its highest level in over a year, which is driving up mortgage rates for homeowners. Daily expenses continue to climb, with taxi and ride-hailing fares surging nearly 50 percent over the last five years, and hawker food prices rising as stallholders pass on higher ingredient costs. 

To cushion these pressures, the government is distributing cost-of-living support. In December, nearly three million adult citizens will receive cash payouts of up to $250 under the final **Assurance Package** disbursement. Meanwhile, retail growth is expected to cool as consumers prioritize value and limit discretionary spending. On a positive note, construction costs are projected to ease to a 3.3 percent growth rate in 2027, which may bring some relief to the property market.

Rising interest rates and persistent daily inflation are squeezing household budgets despite targeted government cash payouts.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The labor market presents a mixed outlook of targeted hiring and structural shifts. Overall employment grew by 27,700 in the third quarter of 2026, but this expansion was accompanied by an increase in retrenchments and resident unemployment. Contract employment is becoming more common as companies seek flexibility in an uncertain economic climate. 

Major multinational corporations continue to anchor their regional operations in Singapore. **Mastercard** announced plans to add over 250 specialist jobs by the end of 2029, and fintech firm **Airwallex** committed $2 billion to expand its local headquarters and double its headcount. However, an **American Chamber of Commerce (AmCham)** survey revealed that while fewer firms plan job cuts, skills mismatch remains a critical challenge for employers. Furthermore, some companies are reducing graduate hiring as artificial intelligence automates entry-level tasks.

The job market is expanding in high-value tech and finance roles, but workers face increased volatility through rising retrenchments and a shift toward contract employment.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is rapidly updating its regulatory frameworks to address environmental, digital, and financial threats. Parliament passed amendments to the **Transboundary Haze Pollution Act**, raising the maximum fine for errant companies to $20 million and allowing trials to proceed even if the accused parties fail to appear in court. This signal is intended to hold offshore polluters accountable before seasonal haze impacts public health.

In the digital sphere, new anti-scam laws are shifting the burden of prevention to service providers. Telecommunication companies will receive broader police powers to block suspicious activities, and a proposed law will introduce default jail terms or reformative training for young scam offenders. Additionally, the **Land Transport Authority (LTA)** has been authorized to retrieve ERP 2.0 vehicle location data for law enforcement and police investigations.

The state is aggressively expanding its regulatory and enforcement powers to police digital security, financial scams, and transboundary environmental threats.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic ties with its immediate neighbors to secure future growth. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a formal framework launch scheduled for December. This initiative has already attracted $28 billion in approved investments and is expected to create over 28,000 jobs, while the **RTS Link** remains on track to open in February 2027.

However, regional political developments are introducing fresh uncertainties. The resignation of Indonesian Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has created political ripples in Jakarta, potentially affecting bilateral policy continuity. At the same time, Malaysia is considering new laws to monitor data center operations and graphics processing unit (GPU) imports, signaling that regional competition for digital infrastructure is tightening.

Singapore is deepening physical and economic integration with Malaysia through the Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone, even as neighboring countries tighten their own technology and resource regulations.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The ongoing technology cold war between the United States and China is directly impacting Singapore's business environment. Following the Nvidia scandal, the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)** barred Alice Huang, the former CEO of Megaspeed, from re-entering Singapore for providing false information to the police. This move signals that Singapore will not allow its reputation for trust and neutrality to be compromised by entities attempting to evade international trade sanctions.

As a result of these tensions, Chinese AI firms are rushing to secure remote chip capacity in Southeast Asia, fearing that Washington will tighten restrictions on cloud providers. Local technology firms and data centers are facing stricter compliance audits. This environment is forcing multinational corporations to review their supply chains and legal structures to ensure they do not run afoul of US export controls.
