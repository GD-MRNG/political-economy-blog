## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singapore is currently navigating a period of environmental and economic tightening. The return of severe transboundary **haze** has degraded air quality, with the 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) reaching unhealthy levels across the island. The government has prepared emergency measures, including mask distribution plans and potential school closures, which are directly impacting daily routines and outdoor activities. 

At the same time, working professionals are feeling a squeeze on their disposable income. While the economy continues to grow, everyday costs like public transport fares and electricity bills are rising. The job market is showing signs of cooling, with retrenchments on the rise even as multinational corporations continue to invest in high-value technology and engineering projects.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost of living in Singapore is becoming heavier as key utility and transport costs rise. Spot electricity prices have hit a high for the year, and public transport fares are increasing despite falling global energy prices in the final quarter. In the housing market, private home prices rose by 1.4% in the third quarter, while HDB resale prices dipped slightly by 0.2%, showing a mixed property market where record-breaking transactions still occur in popular estates like Tanjong Pagar and Bedok. 

The automotive market is in a state of hesitation. The **Land Transport Authority (LTA)** is reviewing the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) system, including a proposed merger of Category A and B, which has caused many buyers to adopt a wait-and-see approach. On the financial front, local banks **DBS**, **OCBC**, and **UOB** experienced a stock sell-off as rising global bond yields forced a reset in market expectations, putting pressure on local equity values. 

Rising utility, transport, and operational costs are squeezing household budgets and small businesses even as asset prices show mixed signals.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

Singapore’s labor market expanded in the second quarter, but this growth was accompanied by a rise in retrenchments to 4,620 cases. The technology and engineering sectors remain bright spots for hiring. **ST Engineering** is creating 500 jobs at its new aviation maintenance facility in Changi Creek, **Google** is accelerating hiring for its local engineering center, and financial technology firm **Revolut** is expanding its headcount with a new central business district office. 

In contrast, traditional consumer-facing sectors are struggling. Retail sales have failed to keep pace with rising tourist arrivals, and several established food and beverage brands have closed outlets due to high rents and manpower shortages. The financial strain is particularly visible among younger workers, with nearly eight in ten Gen Z employees actively seeking additional sources of income to cope with the cost of living. 

The job market is bifurcating, with high-value tech and engineering roles expanding while retail, hospitality, and mid-career professionals face rising retrenchment risks and cost pressures.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant funding and regulatory attention toward future-proofing Singapore's infrastructure and public health. Parliament has passed a new law requiring operators of data centres with a capacity of 3 megawatts or more to be licensed, aiming to manage the country's energy and carbon footprint. To support business transformation, **Enterprise Singapore** is investing S$120 million to boost SME innovation, while the state has launched a S$118 million bioeconomy initiative to develop high-value manufacturing from living organisms. 

Social infrastructure is also receiving a major overhaul. The **Institute of Mental Health (IMH)** will be redeveloped and expanded into the **National Centre for Mental Health Singapore** by 2040 to address growing mental health needs. For families, the government is introducing enhanced, fully-funded childcare leave starting in 2026, alongside digital literacy initiatives like S$7 AI courses run by the **People's Association (PA)** to help citizens adapt to new technologies. 

The state is actively steering the economy toward high-value, regulated digital and green industries while expanding social safety nets for mental health and family support.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic relationship with its neighbors is evolving through both intense competition and closer integration. Malaysia is rapidly building up its technology ecosystem, launching a 2,300-acre "Silicon Island" off Penang to expand its semiconductor and AI industries. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, which has already attracted billions in investment commitments, but the upcoming **Johor Bahru-Singapore RTS Link** is raising concerns that Singaporean retailers could lose significant consumer spending to cheaper businesses in Johor. 

Environmental challenges also require regional coordination. Worsening forest fires in Indonesia have triggered severe haze across Malaysia and Singapore, disrupting flights and forcing school closures in several Malaysian states. Singapore has officially offered assistance to Jakarta to help contain the fires, highlighting the shared vulnerability of the region's economies to environmental disruptions. 

Singapore is deepening economic integration with its neighbors through joint zones and transport links, even as those same neighbors rapidly build up competing high-tech and industrial capabilities.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The global AI revolution is driving a massive data centre boom across the Asia-Pacific region, but this expansion is exposing critical insurance gaps and straining local power grids. Because Singapore may only be able to import a portion of its clean energy targets by 2035 due to regional infrastructure delays, the government is exploring alternative energy sources like Bio-LNG and a potential energy cluster on Pulau Tekong. 

Additionally, geopolitical tensions and trade disputes between the US, China, and Europe are directly impacting local supply chains. US tariffs and export restrictions are forcing multinational technology firms to adjust their regional operations, while Singapore-based wealth managers are preparing for tighter regulatory scrutiny from Beijing regarding offshore trusts.
