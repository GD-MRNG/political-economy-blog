## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singaporeans are currently navigating a physical and financial environment marked by immediate environmental pressures and rising living costs. The return of transboundary haze, with the **Pollutant Standards Index (PSI)** crossing into the unhealthy range in central Singapore, has forced businesses and schools to activate emergency response plans. This physical disruption coincides with a period of high state revenue, as national tax collection reached a record S$97.3 billion, driven by a resilient economy. 

At the same time, household savings are facing sophisticated threats from cross-border financial syndicates. Local authorities are dealing with a surge in complex scams, including a Nanning-based Ponzi scheme that has lured residents with lifestyle incentives, and a major pyramid scheme in China that resulted in the arrest of 52 Singaporeans. These developments are forcing a closer look at personal financial security and digital safety.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Daily living expenses remain high, keeping the cost stack heavy for most households. Core inflation has been driven upward by rising electricity prices. Basic goods are also seeing price pressures, with imported US eggs retailing at up to S$14.80 per dozen. 

The housing market continues to show transaction momentum despite high prices. The removal of the 15-month wait-out period for private property owners downgrading to public flats led to a record 201 million-dollar **Housing & Development Board (HDB)** resale transactions in a single month. In response, the government has raised the income ceilings for **Build-To-Order (BTO)** flats to keep public housing accessible. 

To cushion these pressures, targeted state relief is being rolled out to lower-income families. The government has doubled **Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS)** discounts to 6% at **FairPrice** supermarkets for eligible cardholders. Additionally, community initiatives, such as a new mini-supermarket in Punggol, are distributing S$100 yearly grocery credits to help vulnerable households manage their food budgets. 

*Core dynamic: Rising structural costs in housing and energy are squeezing household budgets despite targeted state relief.*

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market presents a stark divide between high-growth sectors and cost-cutting corporate environments. In the technology and financial sectors, an AI-driven boom is fueling a hiring spree. Fintech firms like **Airwallex** and **Revolut** are actively expanding their headcount, while **JPMorgan** is increasing its local tech talent pool. 

Conversely, broader corporate restructuring is leading to job losses. **CapitaLand Investment** retrenched 90 local staff as part of an organizational restructuring, and **GovTech** has laid off some officers to streamline digital services. These layoffs are occurring alongside a cautious outlook from employers, with a survey by the **Singapore National Employers Federation (SNEF)** showing that over half of local companies plan to freeze or moderate wages in the coming year due to rising manpower costs.

For workers facing transition, the state and labor groups are stepping up support. The **National Trades Union Congress (NTUC)** is providing financial assistance to retrenched members through the **NTUC-U Care Fund**. Meanwhile, career conversion programmes are offering training incentives of up to S$1,600 to help transport workers transition to autonomous vehicle technologies.

*Core dynamic: High-growth AI and fintech sectors are actively hiring, but broader corporate restructuring and employer cost-consciousness are limiting wage growth and job security.*

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant policy attention and funding toward social infrastructure and long-term technological capability. Following the **National Day Rally 2026**, the state is implementing a major reset in family support policies. This includes fully state-funded childcare leave and the introduction of S$500 **Child LifeSG** credits to help parents manage early childhood costs. 

In terms of industrial policy, the government is investing S$800 million in transport research and innovation under the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise 2030 (RIE2030)** plan. This funding is aimed at integrating AI into public transport networks and supporting workers through technological disruptions. 

Furthermore, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** is tightening the regulatory framework for digital finance. The authority has proposed new stablecoin rules covering foreign token recognition and interest bans, while committing S$220 million to accelerate the adoption of frontier financial technologies.

*Core dynamic: The state is aggressively funding family support systems while anchoring long-term economic competitiveness through targeted research grants and digital safety regulations.*

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic ties with its immediate neighbors are deepening through major infrastructure and trade initiatives. The completion of the Woodlands North station marks a key milestone for the **Johor Bahru-Singapore RTS Link**, which is expected to significantly improve cross-border accessibility. Additionally, the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion, with a formal launch projected for March.

However, regional resource constraints are beginning to reshape the industrial landscape. Thailand has frozen 49 data centre projects due to concerns over electricity and water consumption. This regional pause could redirect digital infrastructure demand toward Singapore and Johor, though Singapore's new data centres face strict requirements to use renewable energy. 

Environmental challenges also continue to demand regional coordination. Worsening forest fires in Indonesia have caused severe transboundary haze, prompting Sarawak to declare a haze emergency. The **Singapore Food Agency (SFA)** and other regional bodies are monitoring these environmental impacts closely to safeguard local food and air quality.

*Core dynamic: Deepening physical and economic integration with Malaysia is creating a shared economic corridor, even as regional environmental and resource pressures disrupt daily life.*

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

Global geopolitical tensions and supply chain shifts are directly influencing investment flows into Singapore. To diversify away from geopolitical flashpoints, Taiwan's **United Microelectronics Corporation (UMC)** is investing billions to expand its Singapore fabrication plants. This move aligns with a broader trend of multinational corporations seeking regional stability amid ongoing US-China technology competition.

Global AI consolidation is also driving local capital activity. **Nvidia's** acquisition of **Hugging Face** and confidential IPO filings by Chinese AI firms like **Moonshot** have stimulated the local investment landscape, with Singapore funds recording S$1.3 billion in inflows. However, high global interest rates driven by strong US employment data, alongside elevated energy costs from Middle East conflicts, continue to keep local borrowing and utility costs high.
