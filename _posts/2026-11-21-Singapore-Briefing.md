## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singaporeans are navigating a tightening material reality where daily costs are rising and job security is becoming more fragile. While multinational technology and financial firms continue to expand their footprints, local workers face a more competitive and anxious employment landscape. 

At the same time, the physical and digital infrastructure of the city is undergoing major transitions. The shift to satellite-based road pricing, the redevelopment of public housing estates, and the rising retirement age are forcing residents to adapt to a faster, more expensive, and longer working life.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

The cost stack for everyday living in Singapore is growing heavier. Taxi and ride-hailing fares have surged nearly 50 percent over the last five years, while rising food costs are forcing hawkers to adjust their ingredients. Everyday items like a cup of kopi O now cost up to $1.90, squeezing lower-income and "sandwiched class" families who are struggling to balance spending and saving.

The housing market remains highly competitive, marked by a five-room Build-to-Order (BTO) flat in Dawson setting a resale record of $1.818 million. Although private residential completions surged recently and the **Housing & Development Board (HDB)** launched over 7,900 new flats with higher income ceilings, high land costs keep overall property prices firm. Meanwhile, mortgage rates have risen following global interest rate hikes, adding pressure on homeowners.

To help cushion these pressures, the government is distributing up to $1,500 in **Central Provident Fund (CPF)** top-ups to over 782,000 eligible citizens born in or before 1976. Additionally, the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** is urging banks to manage environmental risks, while consumer financial options are shifting, notably with the announced closure of the **Singlife Account** by December 2027.

Rising structural costs in housing, transport, and food are outpacing wage growth for many residents, despite targeted government financial top-ups.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The job market is experiencing a dual track of high-value tech expansion and localized employment strain. Global firms like **KLA Corporation**, **Mastercard**, and **Mistral AI** are expanding their Singapore offices and creating hundreds of new roles in research, development, and regional operations. However, overall resident unemployment and retrenchments have ticked upward, and fresh graduates are taking longer to secure stable employment.

In response to these pressures, contract jobs are becoming more common, offering flexibility but less long-term security. Traditional sectors, particularly food and beverage, are facing severe headwinds, leading to the closure of long-standing establishments like Penang Place and Tori King. To support lower-wage workers, starting salaries for bus captains will increase by $450 from 2027, and the **National Wages Council** has recommended a 6 to 8 percent built-in wage increase.

High-end technology and multinational hiring remains strong, but local workers face rising retrenchment risks, longer job searches, and a shift toward contract labor.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is directing significant regulatory energy and funding toward national resilience, digital security, and the green transition. Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong announced three **National AI Missions for Financial Services** to establish Singapore as a trusted testbed for artificial intelligence, while **MAS** is developing safeguards for AI-driven payment agents.

On the environmental front, Parliament passed amendments to the **Transboundary Haze Pollution Act**, raising the maximum fine for haze-causing companies to $20 million and allowing trials to proceed in the absence of the accused. The government is also tightening digital safety, introducing new anti-scam laws that grant telecommunication companies broader powers and shifting the burden of security to service providers.

The state is aggressively updating its regulatory frameworks to manage the dual risks of rapid AI adoption and transboundary environmental threats.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore is deepening its economic and infrastructural ties with its immediate neighbors to secure its supply chains and energy needs. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion for a projected March launch, which Malaysia hopes will help mitigate the impact of global trade tariffs.

Cross-border connectivity is also expanding, with **ComfortDelGro** launching a 24-hour taxi service to Johor Bahru and ASEAN leaders signing a memorandum of understanding to enhance the **ASEAN Power Grid**. Meanwhile, Indonesia fully restored its piped natural gas supply to Singapore on November 12, resolving an 18-day disruption and stabilizing local energy inputs.

Singapore is integrating more closely with Malaysia and Indonesia to build regional economic resilience and secure critical energy and labor pipelines.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The intensifying technology rivalry between the United States and China is directly impacting Singapore's position as a neutral business hub. New US export controls on advanced chips are putting pressure on Southeast Asian companies to choose sides, with some **Nvidia** server suppliers already halting shipments to the region. Furthermore, US regulators are targeting the renting of advanced AI computing capacity to Chinese firms in Singapore, which could slow down local data center growth and squeeze Singapore's room to maneuver in the global semiconductor supply chain.
