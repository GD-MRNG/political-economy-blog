## THE GROUND PICTURE

Singapore has officially crossed the threshold into a **super-aged society**, with over 21 percent of the citizen population now aged 65 and older. At the same time, the total population has grown to 6.21 million. This population rise is driven primarily by an influx of foreign construction workers needed for massive infrastructure projects, including Changi Airport Terminal 5 and the expansion of Marina Bay Sands.

Daily life is being squeezed by immediate environmental and financial pressures. The **haze** has returned to the unhealthy range, with 24-hour Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) readings climbing past 100, keeping crowds away from outdoor areas and raising health concerns. Meanwhile, families and small businesses are navigating a high-cost environment where even basic operations require managing steep rental and energy costs.

---

## ECONOMY AND COST OF LIVING

Singapore's core inflation rate rose to 2.2 percent, driven largely by rising service costs. This persistent price pressure has led analysts to expect that the **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** will tighten monetary policy further to stabilize the currency. High interest rates are already pushing up mortgage costs, forcing homeowners to carefully plan their finances as global bond yields hover near 20-year highs.

The housing market remains highly strained. The **Housing and Development Board (HDB)** is reviewing its jumbo flat scheme after a combined unit in Telok Blangah was listed for an unprecedented S$2.18 million, while a Tampines multi-generation flat with 59 years left on its lease sold for S$1.38 million. In the private market, developers are turning pessimistic about suburban residential launches due to record-high pricing and strained buyer affordability.

The food and beverage sector is experiencing intense consolidation. High rental rates, such as a S$10,000 monthly rent reported by a hawker family in Yishun, are making it difficult for small operators to survive while keeping food prices low. Even established brands are struggling, highlighted by the closure of the Italian deli Mamma Mia Focaccia less than a year after opening.

Rising operational and housing costs are outpacing domestic relief measures, keeping the household cost stack heavy.

---

## EMPLOYMENT AND INDUSTRY

The labor market presents a conflicting picture. While the overall employment numbers expanded, retrenchments rose to 4,620 in the second quarter, marking the highest level since 2020. Job vacancies are falling, and retrenched workers, particularly credentialled professionals in their 30s, are finding it increasingly difficult to secure new roles.

Technology and finance remain the primary drivers of corporate activity, but they are undergoing structural shifts. Major firms like **Databricks** and **Plaud** are expanding their Singapore offices, and the semiconductor industry has launched **SG Semiconductor** as a national brand to deepen research and development. However, experts warn that the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence may create an economy that grows with fewer human workers, threatening traditional white-collar roles.

To manage this transition, the government and industry are pushing massive retraining efforts. Twenty-three financial institutions have committed to training 80,000 workers in AI skills by 2028. Meanwhile, companies like **ST Logistics** are actively retraining staff to handle automated warehouse systems as physical automation scales up.

Rising retrenchments and rapid AI integration are creating a highly competitive job market where credentialled workers face growing displacement risks.

---

## GOVERNMENT DIRECTION

The government is focusing its policy and financial resources on long-term demographic resilience and national security. Under the **National Day Rally** initiatives, the government is introducing enhanced childcare leave of up to 12 days and distributing S$500 in Child LifeSG credits to help young families. To address the aging population, the state is launching over 300 cooling centres and testing personalized health dashboards to manage senior wellness.

Regulatory scrutiny is tightening across public and private sectors. The **Public Service Division (PSD)** is investigating property purchases made by rail planning officers between 2007 and 2011 to check for potential insider trading near unannounced MRT stations. Additionally, new laws have been passed to grant police stronger powers to order banks and telecommunications companies to block scam accounts, following millions of dollars lost to impersonation scams.

Public sector compensation is also being adjusted to maintain talent. Ministerial salaries are set to increase by up to 9 percent to S$1.2 million, and Member of Parliament allowances will rise to S$18,500 monthly. The government defended these frameworks in Parliament as necessary to attract high-quality leadership and prevent a drop in administrative standards.

The state is expanding social safety nets for families and seniors while tightening regulatory oversight on public integrity and digital security.

---

## REGIONAL POSITION

Singapore's economic relationship with Malaysia is entering a critical phase. The planning for the **Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ)** is nearing completion for a potential March launch, which Johor officials hope will drive regional development. However, the upcoming **Johor Bahru-Singapore Rapid Transit System (RTS) Link** is expected to divert up to S$1.05 billion in annual consumer spending from Singapore grocers and restaurants to Johor Bahru, where retail prices are significantly lower.

Regional infrastructure and environmental disruptions are also affecting local supply chains. Severe flooding in Bangkok has prompted disaster declarations across all districts, disrupting regional logistics and travel. In response to recurring transboundary haze from Indonesian forest fires, Malaysia is planning to draft its own Transboundary Haze Act, drawing inspiration from Singapore's existing laws.

Closer physical integration with Malaysia offers new investment pathways but threatens to drain retail and food beverage revenues from Singapore's domestic economy.

---

## GLOBAL FORCES LANDING LOCALLY

The recent summit between US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Washington has secured a temporary 100-day trade truce. This detente provides Southeast Asian nations, including Singapore, with a brief window of stability. However, underlying tensions remain high, particularly as US lawmakers push new legislation to block sensitive government systems from using Chinese data center technology, forcing multinational corporations in Singapore to navigate increasingly divided technology supply chains.

Global financial volatility is also hitting local investment vehicles. A global bond sell-off has pushed long-term yields higher, which has dragged Singapore's Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) index toward its 2024 low. This volatile environment is forcing sovereign wealth funds and institutional investors to seek portfolio resilience, leading to major local moves such as **GIC** partnering with **Anthropic** to launch an enterprise AI services firm.
